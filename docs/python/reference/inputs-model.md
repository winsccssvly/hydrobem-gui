# Inputs: the model, the environment and the options

All quantities are SI (metres, kg, s, N); angles are degrees; frequencies are
rad/s. Every input is an item of an object of the model (`hb.Model`), with
exactly one owner and one name (no aliases); the item names below are those of
the engine's schema (`hb.engine.schema()`, `obj.info(item)`), which the model
file keeps (§4). An item is checked when it is set: a value of the wrong type is a
`TypeError`, a value out of its range a `ValueError` naming the object and the
item (`bodies/hull: mass ...`); `update(...)` sets several items all or nothing (a refused value
leaves the object as it was). The tags **[P]**, **[B]**, **[C]** and **[H]**
give the origin of a rule (docs/README.md, Source tags);
untagged rules are HydroBEM's own design.

The model's objects: `m.environment` (§1), `m.options` (§3), `m.first_order` and `m.second_order`
(reference/inputs-orders), the lists `m.meshes` (reference/inputs-meshes), `m.bodies` (reference/inputs-bodies §1) with their children (reference/inputs-bodies §2), `m.lids` and
`m.fixed_dampers` (reference/inputs-model-objects), and `m.tool` (§4).

- **Switches.** Where one of several inputs is used, a switch names it, and the items of the
  choice not made are kept unused: nothing is refused, cleared or reset when a switch or such an
  item is set, and the items the switch leaves out are not read or checked (beyond their shape
  when set). The six switches: `environment.frequency_input` (`omegas` or `periods`, §1), a
  body's `inertia_input` (`Ixx` ... `Izz` or `radii_of_gyration`, reference/inputs-bodies §1.1), `interior_surface`
  (reference/inputs-bodies §2.6), `control_surface` and `control_surface_outer` (reference/inputs-bodies §2.7), and
  `second_order.free_surface_method` (reference/inputs-orders §3). An item of the choice made that is needed and has no
  default, left unset, is refused by `m.check()` and before the solve, naming it
  (`bodies/b: control_surface = 'cylinder' needs control_surface_radius`;
  `bodies/b: control_surface = 'automatic' with control_surface_outer = 'polygon' needs
  control_surface_boundary`; `second_order: free_surface_method = 'mesh' needs
  free_surface_mesh`). There are no dicts and no child objects for the surfaces: the interior
  surface, the control surface and the free surface of the potential QTF are flat items of the
  body and of `m.second_order`, set by assignment or `update()`.
- **Lists.** The objects of a list are made only by `add(name, **items)` and
  `insert(index, name, **items)`; `lst[name]` or `lst[index]`, `del lst[name]`,
  `rename(old, new)`, `move(key, index)`, `names()`, `len()`, iteration and `clear()` (empties the
  list, all or nothing). A list is not assigned (`m.bodies = [...]`, `hull.tanks = []`: refused,
  the message naming `add()` and `clear()`). `add`, `insert` and `update` take the object's own
  items only: a list item such as `dipoles=[...]`, `tanks=[...]`,
  `pressure_modes=[...]` or `pieces=[...]` is refused, and a nested object is added to its
  parent's list once the parent exists (`m.bodies["hull"].dipoles.add(mesh=..., ranges=...)`).
  `obj.items()` gives the own items only (not the name, not the lists), so
  `m.bodies.add("b2", **m.bodies["b1"].items())` copies a body's items. The objects of a list
  keep the defaults of their items.
- `obj.items()` is complete (every own item); `print(obj)` shows the items given that the
  switches choose, then one line naming the items they leave out with the switch value
  (`unused: Ixx ... Izz (inertia_input = "radii_of_gyration"), interior_surface_mesh,
  interior_surface_ranges (interior_surface = "detect"), control_surface_* (control_surface =
  "none")`). The schema (`hb.engine.schema()`, `obj.info(item)`) gives per switched item its
  `switch` (the switch items, space separated) and `switch_values` (the values that take it,
  `"v/w"` for two switches: `control_surface_radius` has the switch `"control_surface
  control_surface_outer"` and the values `"cylinder automatic/circle"`).

## 1. Environment: the water and the waves

The items of `m.environment` (Table 1).

*Table 1. The items of the environment.*

| Item | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `depth` | inf | the water depth $h > 0$, or inf; the sea bed is $z = -h$ |
| `gravity` | 9.80665 | $g$, positive finite; the gravity of a mesh file is not used for the solve |
| `density` | 1000.0 | $\rho$, positive finite |
| `domain` | `"open_sea"` | exactly one of `open_sea`, `x_wall` (wall at $x = 0$), `y_wall` ($y = 0$), `orthogonal_walls`, `channel` (walls $y = \pm W/2$); a body lying wholly in a wall plane is a wavemaker in the wall: radiation of its modes only, no other bodies (theory §10.3) |
| `channel_width` | unset | $W > 0$, only for `channel` (finite depth required) |
| `channel_reflect` | 1.0 | $R$ in 0..1: every wall image of the channel scaled by $R$ (0 the open sea of the same depth); `domain="channel"` only |
| `frequency_input` | `"omegas"` | which of `omegas` and `periods` gives the frequencies; only that side is read (an either-or input, see the introduction): a model that gives periods sets `frequency_input = "periods"` |
| `omegas` | `[]` | with `frequency_input = "omegas"`: the radian frequencies: unique values $\ge 0$, possibly including 0 and +inf, which request the zero- and infinite-frequency radiation limits; the other values are the ordinary frequencies, kept in input order (`Results.omegas`). Limits are never replaced by small/large finite frequencies |
| `periods` | `[]` | with `frequency_input = "periods"`: the wave periods (s), $\ge 0$, 0 the limit $\omega = \infty$ |
| `headings` | `[]` | unique finite propagation directions (degrees, counter-clockwise from +x); required non-empty for diffraction, rejected without it (they would be ignored) |

## 2. Waves

The waves of the solve are given by items of `m.environment` (§1): the frequencies (`omegas`, or
`periods` with `frequency_input = "periods"`) with the limits $\omega = 0$ and $\omega = \infty$, and the headings. The results keep the
ordinary frequencies in input order and the headings as given.

## 3. Options

How the problem is solved, the items of `m.options` (Table 2).

*Table 2. The items of the options.*

| Item | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `higher_order` | False | higher-order method; every body uses a patch mesh |
| `logarithmic_integration` | True | analytic integration of the logarithmic singularity of the wave kernel near the free surface, for the pairs of `log_tolerance` (low order) and in the near tier of the patch integrals (higher order; theory §6, §7); False: plain centroid quadrature of the wave kernel (allowed only without interior surfaces, lids, $z = 0$ pressure planes, free-surface plates or channel) |
| `log_tolerance` | 1e-4 | **[C]** low order: the analytic log integral of a pair is applied where the bound $0.6 (a_j/d)^2$ of the correction it makes (relative to the panel area; $a_j$ the source panel radius, $d = r' + \lvert z + \zeta \rvert$) exceeds this tolerance (theory §6); 0: every pair; must be finite and $\ge 0$ |
| `frequency_threads` | 1 | engine threads over the frequencies, 1..1024; each holds the matrices of one frequency |
| `inner_threads` | 1 | engine threads inside each frequency (assembly, LU), 1..1024; the statics use `frequency_threads × inner_threads`. The results do not depend on either (theory §13.3) |
| `memory_check` | True | before the solve, the resident peak of `m.memory_estimate()` (reference/queries) is compared with the physical memory available at that moment; a solve that needs more is refused with a `ValueError` (Rust `ErrorKind::Value`, C `HB_ERR_VALUE`) giving both and the remedies (fewer `frequency_threads`, fewer panels, a coarser free surface) instead of failing on an allocation, which ends the process; False: solve without the check (theory §13.3) |

## 4. The model file

A model is saved to and read from a model file, `.hbm`: one binary file with every object and
item of the model, the meshes included. It holds the inputs only;
the results file `.hbr` holds the model with its results (reference/results §2), and
`hb.Model.load` reads the inputs of either.

```python
m.save("case.hbm")                     # one file; the folder made
m = hb.Model.load("case.hbm")          # or the inputs of a results file: hb.Model.load("run.hbr")
other = m.copy()                       # a copy of the inputs, without the results
```

- The file keeps every object (the bodies with their surfaces, the lids and fixed dampers, the
  meshes, the tool data) and every item that was given, as given: a mesh read from a GDF file keeps its
  vertices, patches and discretization, so the GDF file is not needed again; numbers keep
  every bit, so a saved model solves to bit-identical results after `hb.Model.load`.
- Reading sets every item again through the engine's checks, so a damaged or edited file is
  refused rather than read wrongly; the file has a CRC-32 for each entry and for its directory
  (the container of reference/results §2). The model record carries its version (13); a file
  of another version is refused with a message naming it (there is no conversion of older
  records).
- `m.tool` holds the data of other programs (a graphical interface, a script) as any UTF-8
  text without NUL characters, kept as given (the list `m.tool`, item `text`).
- An existing file is a `FileExistsError` unless `overwrite=True` (the rules of every writer:
  reference/file-formats §4).
- Meshes are exchanged with other programs as GDF, SPL and CSF files (and the panels of FDF
  files, reference/inputs-orders §3) and as the geometry file
  .hbg (reference/inputs-meshes §5): `m.meshes.load(...)` reads them (reference/inputs-meshes), `mesh.write(...)` writes a `panels`,
  `flat_patches` or `spline_patches` mesh as GDF (and SPL), and a mesh of any kind but
  `trimmed_patches` to a path ending in .hbg;
  `m.meshes.from_hbg(...)` and `mesh.to_hbg()` take and give the same content as Python data.

## 5. Suppressed objects (`enabled`)

The bodies, tanks, dipoles, dipole dampers (of a body and of a tank), fixed dampers, pressure
surfaces, free-surface plates, lids and couplings have the item `enabled` (default true). A disabled object (`enabled = false`) stays in the model and in the model file with every
item; the solve, the rules and the queries of the model take the model as if it were absent, and
the objects under it with it (the sheets of a disabled tank, everything of a disabled body):

- a coupling that names a disabled body is left out;
- the field points of a disabled tank, or of a tank of a disabled body, are left out with it (the
  tank is no owner of the results, reference/inputs-model-objects §3); with no point left in the sea or in a tank there are no
  field outputs (a field output switched on without points is refused, reference/inputs-rules §1 rule 1);
- a lid placed with a disabled body is refused (it is placed by that body): disable it too, or
  give it in global coordinates;
- every body disabled is refused (`every body is disabled`), as a model without bodies;
- the rules count the enabled objects (`body_points` for every enabled body or none, a patch mesh
  for every enabled body with `options.higher_order`, ...);
- names stay unique among all objects, disabled ones included, and a mesh a disabled object uses
  is still in use (it cannot be removed); a reference to a disabled object stays valid.

A model with a disabled object gives the results of the model without it, file for file. The
bodies of the results, their coordinates and the bodies of the numeric files are numbered over
the enabled bodies (1, 2, ...); `results.bodies["x"]` of a disabled body is a `KeyError` that says
so, and `results.model` holds every object with its `enabled` at the time of the solve. The
locations of the errors and warnings (`Location.body`, `lid`, `field_point`) are those of the
model, disabled objects counted, and a message names an unnamed object by its place in the model
(`tank 2` with the first tank disabled).

`m.check()` checks the model as it is solved, and then each disabled object as if it alone were
